Improving Course Registration for University and College Students with Unmet Expectations

Role
UX Researcher
Institution
University of Washington
Focus
Course registration · Unmet student expectations
Methods
Survey

Background

This user research studies how to improve course registration experiences for university and college students who have unmet course registration expectations. By examining the course registration journey, we seek to understand what actions students take—if any—to meet their expectations, as well as what strategies prove effective or ineffective.

Context

We plan to identify the pain points students face when their course registration expectations are not met, as well as what strategies—if any—they are currently using or have used to better meet those expectations. I am interested in learning about what a typical course registration experience looks like and what makes it a positive or negative experience.

We will focus on the following questions:

  • What do students with unmet expectations do to better meet them?
  • What types of expectations do different students hold about course registration?
  • How do the course registration experience vary based on the expectations that they have?
  • What does a typical registration experience look like for students with unmet expectations?
  • Under what circumstances do students feel they have a good registration experience?
  • Do students use tools, apps, or services to support their registration process?
  • How do students prepare for course registration?
  • How do students handle the challenges that arise when course registration does not go as planned?
  • How does a recent registration experience influence a student's approach to the next one?

Goals

  1. Goal 1

    To evaluate the effectiveness and ease of the course registration experience for people who have unmet course registration expectations.

  2. Goal 2

    To identify pain points and areas for improvement in the course registration experience for people who have unmet course registration expectations

  3. Goal 3

    To determine whether students find any tools, apps, or services helpful for course registration

Research questions

  1. 01

    What is the course registration experience like for students who have unmet course registration expectations?

  2. 02

    What characterizes a positive course registration experience for students? What factors contribute to a negative experience?

  3. 03

    Is there anything that students are currently doing to improve their course registration experience or better meet their expectations?

Target User Group(s)

Including criteria

  1. 1 Current U.S. college/university students who are sophomores (second year) or above, as well as recent graduates
  2. 2 Students who have recently experienced challenges with course registration
  3. 3 Students from diverse demographics (gender, age, major, year)

Excluding criteria

  1. 1 First-years / Freshman
  2. 2 Non-university students
  3. 3 Students attending a university outside the U.S.
  4. 4 University staff, faculty, or administrator

Survey

Following the research plan, a survey was distributed to U.S. college and university students who had recently experienced course registration challenges. Questions covered openness to new planning tools, resources currently used, what helps or hinders schedule building, and optional open-ended responses.

Survey Content

Would you be open to using a new course planning system? *

  • Yes
  • No

What resources do you currently use for course registration or planning? *

  • Degree audit / planning tool
  • Official registration system
  • Academic advisors
  • Course descriptions
  • Online guides
  • Peer advising
  • Workshops
  • Orientation
  • Instructor guidance

What has been helpful when creating your schedule? *

  • University-provided tools
  • Advice from people
  • Third-party apps
  • Self-created tools
  • Nothing has been helpful

What makes course planning EASY for you? *

  • Degree requirements availability
  • Time conflict tools
  • View all sections
  • Variety of times / formats
  • Advisor input
  • Familiarity with the system

What makes course planning DIFFICULT for you? *

  • Course time conflicts
  • Availability of required / preferred courses
  • Prerequisites
  • Limited variety of times / formats
  • Course descriptions availability
  • Schedule changes
  • Advising access

Anything else you'd like to add about your course registration experience?

Findings

12 participants completed the survey.

Students are open to better planning tools

Nearly everyone expressed willingness to try a new course planning system — 11 of 12 respondents (91.7%) said they would be open to one, while only 1 of 12 (8.3%) was not. This suggests that frustration with current registration workflows has not made students resistant to change; instead, most are actively looking for something that works better.

Degree tools and official systems anchor the workflow

Students rely heavily on university-provided infrastructure. Degree audit or planning tools were universal — every respondent (12/12, 100%) used one. The official registration system followed closely (11/12, 91.7%), and academic advisors (10/12, 83.3%) and course descriptions (9/12, 75%) rounded out the core toolkit. Supplementary resources had more modest reach: online guides (6/12, 50%), peer advising and workshops (4/12 each, 33.3%), orientation (3/12, 25%), and instructor guidance (2/12, 16.7%).

University tools and human advice drive successful scheduling

When asked what actually helps them build a schedule, university-provided tools led at 11 of 12 (91.7%), followed by advice from people at 9 of 12 (75%). Third-party apps and self-created tools each drew 3 of 12 (25%), and 2 of 12 (16.7%) said nothing had been helpful — underscoring that most students do find support, but a small minority feel entirely on their own.

Clear requirements and scheduling visibility ease planning

What makes planning feel easy centers on knowing what you need and seeing your options. Degree requirements availability topped the list at 11 of 12 (91.7%). Time conflict tools, viewing all sections, variety of times and formats, and advisor input each appeared in 8 of 12 responses (66.7%). Familiarity with the system was a moderate factor at 7 of 12 (58.3%) — suggesting that even when tools exist, the learning curve still matters.

Conflicts and availability create the most friction

Course time conflicts dominated what makes planning difficult — 10 of 12 respondents (83.3%) selected them. Availability of required or preferred courses and prerequisites tied at 7 of 12 (58.3%) each. Limited variety of times or formats (5/12, 41.7%) and course descriptions availability (5/12, 41.7%) followed. Schedule changes (4/12, 33.3%) and advising access (2/12, 16.7%) appeared less often, but the top three pain points all relate to structural constraints students cannot easily resolve on their own.

Students develop workarounds when systems fall short

Three respondents shared open-ended strategies. One described using UW seat-notification and waitlist alerts to get into full classes — a reactive tactic when preferred sections fill before registration opens. Others emphasized checking DARS and degree requirements alongside department projected offerings to plan ahead. Several noted asking professors and older students for course recommendations, reinforcing the survey pattern that human guidance complements — but does not replace — official tools.
