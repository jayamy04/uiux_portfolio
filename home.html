About

I am a UX researcher with a background in computational cognitive science, which is the study of the brain, psychology, and computer science, and I also double majored in interaction design. I started out focused on web and app design and then decided to pursue UX research after working on a study about how blind and low vision people want to discover and experience public art, during my Master’s program in Human Centered Design and Engineering at the University of Washington. I still love design and do design work, but I fell for how open research is, and how talking to people can solve real problems.

After grad school, I took a contract role at Material, which is a research and design consulting company that helps major brands and innovative companies grow, where I supported research on a B2B marketing tool, making sure businesses have a smooth and efficient experience using a marketing platform.

As I continue in my UX research career, I see myself growing in the exploratory research space that cares about learning from people’s real experiences to shape the product direction.

Projects

Nordstrom Rack “Buy Online & Pick Up in Store” Research

Evaluative research

Angela, a Principal UX researcher at Nordstrom, spotted the same- or next-day pickup filter failing—and it had never been tested with real shoppers. I helped define the parameters, recruit participants, facilitate sessions, and report findings to her UX team. Across 8 remote usability tests with shoppers near Nordstrom Rack stores, one question drove the work: how easily can users navigate the desktop site to find and purchase same- or next-day pickup items?
