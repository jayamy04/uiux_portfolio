EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Generative AI is helping students save time through idea generation, editing emails and assignments, creating outlines for research papers, and explaining new, complex concepts. AI is used frequently inside classrooms, helping students finish their homework more efficiently with less stress. In this research report, we are exploring how students–specifically in the healthcare field–are using AI to increase their productivity in their study process.

Compared to other students, healthcare students have a lot more study materials. To gain knowledge in their study habits, we first did secondary research to see if AI is used by healthcare students, how it is incorporated into their study routine, and what AI tools are used. Then, we performed user interviews with 7 healthcare students and observed their study habits in the health sciences library to gather qualitative and ethnographic data. Then, we did data analysis on Excel to organize data, identify patterns, and establish codes and themes to validate participant responses. Then, we found that most healthcare students use generative AI to simplify study materials and generate new resources like practice exams and flashcards. On the contrary, we identified that students retain information for a longer period of time when studying with traditional study materials such as pen and paper. Also, we found that due to inaccurate and unreliable answers provided by Gen AI, med students often cross reference with trusted sources like UpToDate and medical institution websites. Another finding is that AI lacks a human aspect when it comes to diagnosing patients in real life situations which leads students to believe that AI will only support, rather than replace, them in the future. Therefore students rely on their own notes, slides and textbooks given by professors and other trusted sources to overcome these limitations.

RESEARCH MOTIVATION AND BACKGROUND

The purpose of this study is to learn about the study routines of healthcare students and assess the usefulness of generative AI in their study process. Being a student in the healthcare field is difficult and requires time management skills, resilience, and memorization. They spend most of their time studying, taking exams, or interacting with patients. We wanted to study how they manage to have time for breaks, hanging out with friends and family, etc.

Through our secondary research, we found that AI acts like a study buddy that helps them generate USMLE-type questions, flashcards, and virtual patients. It also helps pinpoint important information from textbooks so students take less time reading and more time learning [1]. AI tools like Quizlet, ChatGPT, and Notion AI help study for exams [2]. Also, a research article stated that students depend on ChatGPT too much [5]. Lastly, we were curious about how AI can help or hinder patient and doctor relationships [6]. These findings led us to think that students in healthcare have positive feelings about using AI to enhance their study process.

Our research question

We narrowed down our questions to focus on the perception of gen AI because we learned that a lot of students do not use AI at all or very minimally. This led us to challenging our assumptions that all students perceive AI positively and dig deeper into why AI is not useful or ethical depending on their situation.

Methods

4.1. Secondary research

We started by doing secondary research to equip ourselves with the knowledge and vocabulary to approach healthcare students. We wanted to understand how generative AI was currently integrated in healthcare studies and tools that were specific to the healthcare domain. Each person generated main trends from our findings.

Data collection procedure

Our initial data collection was both online and in-person.

  • 1We searched through YouTube videos and Reddit posts in r/medschool, r/OpenAI, and r/medicine and searched for keywords such as AI education, AI in med education, and AI in medicine. We also read through articles posted on healthcare and medical school portals. We summarized and pasted direct quotes from the posts.
  • 2We undertook a competitive analysis of generative AI tools used in the field of healthcare.
  • 3We walked around the health sciences library to observe how students are using AI and whether they use AI in their learning.

Data Analysis

With our main trends, we grouped them through affinity diagramming to extract main themes to focus on for our research:

  • 1The med student learning experience is personalized and not one-size-fit-all; it is based on preferred study methods like active recall, note-taking, and visual learning.
  • 2The med student learning process is increasingly nonlinear, students toggle between different platforms and both online and offline to find the information they need.
  • 3Med students heavily rely on textbooks to generate information, AI and search engines act as supplementary tools that fail to replace more traditional study methods
  • 4Med students don’t utilize AI as much in comparison to non med students, suggesting that med students are not yet aware of or new to the benefits of Gen AI.

4.2. Ethnographic Study

Due to the lack of response received for the screening survey we decided to undertake ethnographic study in the health science library. This proved to be very helpful as we could observe students in their natural environments.

Data collection procedure

We visited the University of Washington's library of health science. We began by observing students while they were in their natural habitat studying. We spoke to students who seemed approachable (transitioning between classes, taking a break from studies, etc.) and spoke to them about our research. We shared a screening survey and requested to be shared among their peers.

Data analysis

  • 1We engaged with students from pharmacy, dental, nursing, and other healthcare fields, realizing that ‘medical students’ was too narrow a term for our target group.
  • 2Students rely a lot on collaborative studying methods. Students were observed studying using physical models (skeletons, dental kits, etc.)
  • 3Studying is a major part of the initial school years as majority students in the library were from 1st - 3rd years.
  • 4Healthcare students have a busy schedule as students were seen transitioning between library - classes - clinical practices.

4.3. Screening survey

The screening survey helped us gather data from a large pool of healthcare students.

Data collection procedure

We shared our screening survey with

  • 1Students in the healthcare library, requested them to share with their peers and post it on their workspaces.
  • 2Posted on UW slack channels
  • 3Posted on Reddit

Data analysis

  • 1We received total of 53 responses
  • 227 students who filled our screening survey use generative AI
  • 3Studying is mainly done by students in early years as 42 responses received were from students in 1st, 2nd and 3rd year.
  • 4Notion (26), Anki (16), Quizlet (16) in addition to ChatGPT are most commonly used AI applications

4.4. Interviews

We conducted interviews to gain a deeper understanding of the effectiveness of generative AI in the healthcare study process.

Data collection procedure

Although 13 participants in the screening survey agreed to participate in the interview only 7 interviewed. We conducted a total of 7 interviews, 5 online and 2 in person in the lobby of the Health Sciences Library at The University of Washington. Our participants were from different healthcare fields like medical, dental, pharmacy and even pre-medical students. We had to ensure our interviews were flexible to accommodate the bust schedule of healthcare students.

Our interviews consisted of our introduction, project overview, recording consent, combination of descriptive, structural and contrast questions, ending note and request to connect with potential interviewees.

Our interviews were conducted with one team member with 0 or 1 moderator. We would take notes while conducting and moderating the interviews. We would regroup and discuss the interview process and discuss how to approach the next interviews. Then, we would transcribe the recordings and add it to our excel where other team members could read. The same excel was used to find relevant codes and themes.

Data analysis

The interviews provided us with many findings that we further synthesized through the medium of affinity diagram and codebook creation to arrive at insights.

Key insights and findings

After analyzing our interviews, we identified recurring patterns among participants and documented them as codes. Using affinity mapping and triangulation analysis (combining data from our screening survey, ethnographic study, and interviews), we categorized these codes into overarching themes. This process led to the identification of 6 themes, 3 positive and 3 skeptical.

Positive

Theme 1 (Positive)

TIME SAVING

Generative AI helps healthcare students save time.

Code 1: Hectic

‘I mean, our schedules are a bit hectic where there's not like one singular heavy day, there's like multiple heavy days.’ - Participant A

Code 2: Packed

“Classes can start as early as 7:30 AM and end at 4:30-5:00 PM, but some students stay until 7:00 PM to practice and then study after that.” - Participant D

“The first two years of medical school had a structured schedule with morning classes, practice questions, and self-study. In the third year, there are heavy study days including long clinic hours followed by studying for rotation exams.” - Participant E

Code 3: Information Overload

“I think it was just the quantity of material & organizing it to make it easy to look back on. There is just such a large volume so sometimes I get lost” - Participant F

Code 4: Notes Summarization

“Notion AI helps save time by answering a question by summing it up from my notes.” - Participant B

Code 5: Efficient

“ I think the less time you spend looking up information and watching videos, the more time you can use to practice the hard skills and answer actual questions.” - Participant G

Positive

Theme 2 (Positive)

SIMPLIFICATION

Generative AI helps simplify complex topics

Code 1: Helpful

“So I just asked the AI to explain to me what the cross-section of a placental unit would look like?.” - Participant B

Code 2: Time saving

“AI makes finding things quicker...you bet you're able to get over that page a lot quicker than if you had to..." - Participant B

“It's like, it saves time and then it just helps me study when I'm in a time crunch." - Participant B

Code 3: Better Language

“This prompt is basically my notes. And I just add the prompt, like here you'll see, like explain or like rewrite, you know, just to like see if it can do it in a better language." - Participant B

Code 4: Tables

“And this, I had specifically asked to put in a table. I said, make a table under two columns and I want it this way. And so just put it, the effects of elevated estrogen and progesterone in like two different tables for me.” -Participant B

Code 5: Summarization

“...it makes it into a format that we can absorb more easily, like instead of just like reading like blocks of text, especially for, like um psychology and sociology, because there's like the 80 page document, which is a lot, so it can kind of like condense that on like smaller chunks, but like chapter by topic, um, and it makes it look a little bit easier to remember” - Participant C

Positive

Theme 3 (Positive)

RESOURCE GENERATION

Generative AI helps healthcare students generate resources

Code 1: Prompt Generation

“I think their prompts are more like summarizing this entire article for me and giving me five bullet points that may be asked on a quiz." - Participant B

Code 2: Information Expansion

“I think I did a question bank set. I used it because I wanted to expand on a couple of specific concepts that were being described. In general I found it kind of useful.” - Participant G

Skeptical

Theme 4 (Skeptical)

LACK OF TRUST

Healthcare students feel lack of trust in the content generated by Generative AI

Code 1: Lack of “Human Aspect”

The participant mentions the human aspect when diagnosing a patient that Gen AI cannot accommodate for: "there is a human aspect behind those patients and it takes a human to see that, I don't think it sees the chart the way we do” -Participant B

Code 2: Unreliable Information

The participant noticed AI-generated blurbs in Google and Bing search results summarizing medical topics. However, they do not find them useful, as they often simplify concepts too much for professional use. They rely on verified sources like UpToDate and medical institution websites rather than AI-generated content, which they find lacks precision in medical terminology. — Participant E

“...These days google images is, I think, at least for like medical stuff. It's very unreliable, so you can't just look up an image and be like, oh, like, is this what it looks like, right?" - Participant B

Code 3: Inaccurate Answers

“oh, like I think this is like ADP and AMP, when it should have just been in a DP. And then I was like, okay, maybe I shouldn't use this because it's giving me wrong answers, but yeah, that's like a pretty, like big mistake. Yeah, so that's what I was kind of like maybe I shouldn't use this” - Participant C

Skeptical

Theme 5 (Skeptical)

CROSS REFERENCING

Healthcare students feel the need to cross verify the content generated by Generative AI

Code 1: Cross Check

The participant mentions cross-checking AI responses: "But I do sort of cross-check that” -Participant B

Code 2: Reference Trusted Databases

The participant primarily uses UpToDate for medical conditions, diagnostic procedures, and treatments. They also cross-reference Google searches with trusted sources like university-affiliated websites and major medical institutions like the Mayo Clinic. — Participant E

Skeptical

Theme 6 (Skeptical)

INFORMATION RETENTION

Healthcare students feel their information retention capacity is reduced when using generative AI as information is easily handed to them and most studies are memorization based.

Code 1: Paper and Pen

“I've been using paper and pen to remind myself of information that I had forgotten. The physical act of writing is helpful.” - Participant G

“I think just my study style... I prefer to draw the things out myself and write things down myself. I can remember better that way” - Participant F

Code 2: Long Term Memory

“It's so hard to remember the information it gives me, because I didn't do any work to acquire that information. “ - Participant B

“But I do feel dumber. I do feel a little bit like, you know, like I'm not, my brain is not being used to its full potential.” - Participant B

Refer Affinity Diagram and Interview codes for codebook workspace.

The themes further helped us arrive at insights.

  1. Insight 1

    Healthcare students need to use time efficiently, hence they use generative AI to simplify study material whilst generating new resources.

  2. Insight 2

    Due to the lack of trust in the inaccurate and unreliable answers provided by Gen AI, med students often rely on cross referencing from trusted sources.

  3. Insight 3

    Most students still rely on traditional study materials, such as pen and paper, to ensure proper information retention and improve long term memory.

Impact of our findings

Future Application Of Research

Through our various primary and secondary research methods, we generated a list of deliverables that could be used to guide future design recommendations.

  • Competitive Analysis of 9 different Gen AI products used in the healthcare industry (ie. Amboss, Quizlet, ChatGPT)

    This is helpful because it allows tech companies to identify market trends, and differentiate their product because knowing what features and functionalities competitors provide helps in identifying unique selling points that can set a product apart.

  • 3 insight statements to provide reasoning for healthcare students’ studying behavior

    These statements provide straightforward guidance for companies to progress forward. They can also help frame design goals when it comes to brainstorming new product features.

  • Quotes from different healthcare students on their perception of different Gen AI tools.

    These quotes are evidence that tech companies can reference in the future when they are making those important product decisions. These quotes provide direct, human-centered insights into the needs and wants of these students and can help stakeholders empathize with their motivations and frustrations.

  • A comprehensive codebook & visual diagram of research data

    The diagram can help tech companies draw connections between interviewee sentiments. The comprehensive codebook acts as a reference guide for these tech companies when deciding which features and ideas to prioritize.

Broad Recommendations

Educate Stakeholders

Since Gen AI is still new to the medical/ healthcare field, I would suggest specific product teams go to different medical schools to talk to the students and professors, educating them on the availability and benefits of Gen AI. Without awareness or knowledge of these tools, students won’t integrate it into their daily study routines and product reach will be limited. Marketing on social media would also assist as students are more prone to using tools their friends are using!

Educate yourselves

With the accurate knowledge of healthcare terminology and platforms students use, these tech companies can build trust and credibility when interacting with the student. Being the ones designing products, it is important to ask the right questions and enhance communication efficiency whilst uncovering real pain points that might be overlooked without prior knowledge.

Specific Product Recommendations

  • AI Diagram Generation on flash cards

    Drawn from Insight 1: Healthcare students need to use time efficiently, hence they use generative AI to simplify study material whilst generating new resources.

    “Since we're in the science field and we have to draw a lot for our field, we use Adobe Illustrator to create those images. If Gen AI could generate that, it would be helpful. " - Participant F

    one participant commented on the importance of visual cues during their study process, how they manually create diagrams on Adobe Illustrator, which becomes tedious over time. This idea introduces AI as a time saving tool which generates diagrams relevant to the specific flashcard’s content?

  • Syncing Notion AI with Anki

    Drawn from Insight 1: Healthcare students need to use time efficiently, hence they use generative AI to simplify study material whilst generating new resources.

    “I think it was just the quantity of material & organizing it to make it easy to look back on. There is just such a large volume so sometimes I get lost” - Participant F

    “Notion AI helps save time by answering a question by summing it up from my notes.” - Participant B

    Many interviewees described the difficulty in locating certain phrases in their large abundance of curated notes, which Notion AI does successfully, according to one participant. What if Notion AI could sync with Anki or Quizlet to streamline the flashcard creation process- from digging through chapters of notes to find key terms, to transferring them onto flashcards, what if this could be turned into a one step process?

  • Different Information Modalities

    Drawn from Insight 3: Most students still rely on traditional study materials, such as pen and paper, to ensure proper information retention and improve long term memory.

    “…there's like a thing where you can like scan your notes and it makes it a podcast. So i feel like that would be kind of helpful and it would save time because I can listen to it when I’m on the bus… ” - Participant E

    This recommendation came directly from one participant who suggested that if notes were heard, it would both save time and help develop long term memory of study material, improving information retention and benefiting students during exam season.

Future work and reflection

If we had more time…

  • 1We would interview secondary stakeholders. In this case, they are the healthcare instructors who create the curriculum these students follow. This is important because they help facilitate these students’ learning journeys and understand what is needed to succeed.
  • 2We would also conduct another even longer ethnographic study to observe students in their natural studying environment. This was crucial to test our assumptions and see them interact with their study material without verbal feedback or the pressure of an interview.
  • 3We would reach out to designers from tech companies like Anki and ChatGPT to understand their thought processes behind creating more successful features. This would act like an in-depth competitive analysis.

If we could do it again…

  • 1We would incorporate contextual inquiries into the interview process because it was really helpful when one participant demonstrated what prompt she’d feed ChatGPT to generate the ideal response. They also walked us through Notion AI, which allowed us to see how different platforms serve different purposes. By conducting contextual inquiries, we could catch behavioral details on their interactions with those Gen AI platforms.
  • 2We would scout for interviewees earlier on in the semester. Our schedule was a bit rushed towards the end because we lacked available interview participants during the middle of the project timeline. Had we gone to screen people in person earlier on in the semester, maybe we could have gathered more insights!

Limitations

  • 1Time constraints: If we had more time, we would have recruited more med students, professionals, or professors rather than pre-med students which may have led to more diverse insights
  • 2Lack of resources: We did not have a connection with the UW med department which made recruitment difficult. We were not sure what groups to reach out to even with the email listings on their website
  • 3Lack of connections with clubs: Most clubs on campus are run by undergrads so as grad students where we primarily work with each other, did not have connections with clubs that possibly had connections with med students and schools.
