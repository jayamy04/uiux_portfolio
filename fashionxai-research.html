Course
HCDE 534
Role
UX Researcher
Institution
University of Washington
Goal
understand pain points around purchasing clothing online when it comes to size and fit
Methods
Netnography · Secondary research

Background

FITTED is a mobile app designed to reduce the high volume of returns in online clothing shopping by helping users find clothes that fit as expected. Returns account for over 40% of all online clothing purchases which has increased by 15% since 2023. Not only is this costly for retailers, but it also contributes to environmental waste, as most returned clothes end up in landfills, not back on shelves.

Through user research, we found the main three reasons for returns: 1. Sizing inconsistencies across brands 2. Item listings lack essential garment measurements 3. Fit is subjective

As a result, shoppers often stick with familiar brands because they know what size works for them. Trying new brands or dressing for new activities means starting from scratch, which creates a barrier for them.

Secondary research

In online shopping, high clothing return rates are a big issue that needs to be solved. One of the primary reasons for this is due to incorrect sizing. To address this issue, our team explored a solution to help online shoppers find the right size and fit of clothing they purchase online to mitigate the return problems.

3 Problems

  1. 1

    Lack of Industry-Wide Measurement Standards - what exactly is a sleeve length? How do I measure sleeve length?

  2. 2

    Fit Is Subjective - One person's true to size is another persons' baggy

  3. 3

    Technical Limitations - It's just hard to do at scale ~ time consuming/expensive; color can be decieving

Customer segmentation

The core users of our shops are people who want to experiment with fashion, but traditionally don’t fit into the pre-existing paradigms and categories created by retail companies.

These customers typically experience 3 main core issues.

Findings

Sizing categories are inconsistent across different vendors

Sizing categories are inconsistent across different vendors to a single larger retail distributor. “Inconsistent sizing across brands. A 10 should be universally the same across all brands. It’s infuriating and a waste of time.”

Body measurements are displayed in a vague or scheming manner

Body measurements are displayed in a vague or scheming manner to sell items. “The model wearing the asos petite was apparently 5’3. I find it insane that the average woman’s height is 5’4 yet making pants that fit women who are 5’3 maybe 5’2 is considered inclusive sizing.”

Fit is subjective and misaligned across different retailers

Fit is subjective and misaligned across different retailers. “Size charts tend to put me 1–2 sizes larger than I would have picked in person. I'm not sure why this is.” Size charts put 1-2 sizes larger than in person. Generic size charts don't account for fit preferences.

Quality/fit uncertainty online

Shoppers asked how they know an item will fit before buying. “When you shop for clothes online, how do you know the size you need? How do you know if an item's fit or cut will look good on you, even if the size it right?” “How do you know if an item’s fit or cut will look good on you, even if the size is right?” “I buy brands I trust or if I'm taking a risk brands that can be returned easily” “You can never be 100% sure, but reviews and sizing charts help. I also won't ever buy online if the return policy is bad, just in case.”

Taking measurements, size guides, preferred silhouettes

Taking measurements, size guides, preferred silhouettes. “Your body measurements only tell you the smallest size you can fit into, not the ideal size of clothing you should wear” “Your body measurements only tell you the smallest size you can fit into...” Compare with clothes you already own.

Size charts don't always have enough information

Size charts don't always have enough information. “size charts unfortunately often just take the average measurements for that size/brand rather than being unique to each item”

Fashion-Enthusiast on a Budget

This persona heavily values high quality and niche brands, but does not have the funds to purchase a lot of these boutique items. The brands they typically buy from do not have the same level of customer support and operations to maintain and support a post transaction economy. “I buy from stores that have a good return policy and detailed garment measurements”

The Skeptic

This persona has had multiple bad experiences in the past with mismatched item configurations. They will spend hours on end in decision paralysis, only to be an item they are unsatisfied with. They are even more disappointed taking time out of their day to return their items. This creates high risk-aversion and limited wardrobe users who are represented as a loss in potential sales. “I read reviews and stick to brands I know. If it comes from Amazon it’s going to be cheap. If it comes from a major dept store or target it will be mediocre. If it comes from lines that only sell in boutiques or a well known website it will be better.”

Time Cruncher

This persona is typically extremely busy. They do not have time to deal with returns. They just need something that works. They express the worst segment of their user journey is the returns process. “Lol it's so annoying to return most of the things I buy. I have to spend time on it, and I get my money back weeks after that.”

Behavioral patterns from secondary research

From the secondary research, we've identified common patterns in customers' behavior and pain points that users face in their online shopping experience. The first tendency is that shoppers stick with familiar brands because they know their size. For them, trying new brands or dressing for new activities means starting from scratch, so many users don't want to bother to try new clothes, worried about the potential that the clothes might not fit their body size.

Another interesting behavior we identified is the “Bracketing” phenomenon, which means the practice of online shoppers purchasing multiple items to try different sizes, colors, or styles to get the best clothing item that they are satisfied with. Even though it is obvious that this practice can cause a high return rate, companies take this approach because they can be competitive among their competitors by offering free returns and exchanges, which provides benefits to their customers.

The other interesting pattern we've seen in customers' behavior is the ease of online shopping and consumers' impulse purchase of clothing. It is getting much easier than ever before to purchase clothes online with just a few clicks. However, this ease can prevent consumers from carefully considering whether the items are what they really need. The chances are that the items don't meet their expectation, which can lead to high returns.

Workarounds

Workarounds: measurements, compare with owned clothes, reviews, order 3 sizes. Good return policy + detailed garment measurements. Try in store then buy online.

Can't try on online, returns wasteful

Can't try on online, returns wasteful. Petite sizing issues. out of 200ish items, ~30 needed alteration, ~40 unwearable.

Research conclusion

Based on our research, we concluded that the ideal online cloth shopping experience requires a combination of personal recommendations based on user purchase history, preferences, and biometrics, and also in recommendations curated based on what similar profiles identify as personal fit and style.

As discovered in our customer research, we identified that many online shoppers experience sizing issues in the existing platform because the sizing chart/measurement is insufficient, sizing is inconsistent across different brands, and size is subjective depending on different types of shoppers. This proves that there is a demand for a reliable recommendation tool for sizing, and there is a gap between the demand and the lack of such a sizing recommendation tool.
